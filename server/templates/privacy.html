What we keep, why and on what legal basis

Your account, to provide the service you sign up for (contract). We keep the identifier your sign-in service gives us (your ORCID iD, or your GitHub or GitLab account number), which is how we recognise you next time; an internal account name generated at random (seen by the owners of projects you edit and by us); when you joined and last signed in; and which projects you may edit. A draft is a project form you have saved but not yet submitted; we keep it so you can come back to it. If you enable an authenticator, we keep its encrypted setup secret, recovery-code data and usage dates. We discard the provider's profile and its access tokens; your password stays with ORCID, GitHub, GitLab or, where offered, an institutional sign-in.{% if email_login_enabled %} If you sign in with email or add an email address, we keep it for verification and account access; it is never public.{% endif %}

Cookies, only those needed to run the service: a sign-in session cookie (up to 14 days), a form-protection cookie (up to one year) and a short-lived cookie for status messages. There is no "remember this browser" cookie, and no analytics or advertising.

Security, in our legitimate interest in keeping the service safe. Rate limits use keyed digests, not readable IP addresses. Our own request log keeps only the time, method, status and size of each request.

Projects, in the legitimate interest of contributors and readers in accurate, lasting scholarly credit. Drafts and pending submissions are visible only to their owner, editors and us. An accepted listing shows the title, description, contributor names, ORCID iDs, DOI, repository link and any website. Every submitted revision and review decision is kept as a record of what was approved. Each contributor's institutional email is never published: only the project's owner, its editors and we can see it, and we use it to confirm the submission to that contributor and to contact them about the listing. Only list contributors you are authorised to name; an ORCID iD in a contributor list does not give that person access.

Repository authorization, in our legitimate interest in publishing only projects that their submitters may list. We keep the repository and owner identifiers, your GitHub account identifier, login and permission level or the commit containing your verification file, any connected ORCID iD, and dates. This evidence is private. A verification file in a public repository is public there, including in its history. When you verify, submit or a project is approved, we query GitHub about that public repository; a GitHub authorization is used once and not stored.

Privacy requests and security incidents, to meet our legal obligations.

We have checked that these legitimate interests do not override your privacy: the information is limited to what credit and security need. We do not market to you, sell your information or make automated decisions about you. You need not give us anything, but you cannot submit a project without signing in.

If someone else named you

A submitter can list you as a contributor. We then receive your name, institutional email and any ORCID iD from them, not from you, and use them as described under Projects. When the project is submitted for review, we email you to say that you were listed, with a link to this notice. You have the same rights as account holders, including the right to object, and you do not need an account to use them.

How long we keep it

  • Sign-in sessions last up to 14 days. Editor invitations expire after 7 days. Repository checks expire after 10 minutes (GitHub) or 24 hours (verification file), and a completed authorization is valid for 30 days.{% if email_login_enabled %} Email sign-in codes expire after 3 minutes.{% endif %}
  • An hourly cleanup deletes expired sessions, and invitations and unused checks 30 days after they end.
  • Drafts never submitted are deleted after 90 days without edits, invitations or repository checks. Opening a page does not count.
  • Accounts with no projects or other responsibilities are deleted after 12 months without a sign-in, without a reminder.{% if email_login_enabled %} Unverified email signups are deleted after 7 days.{% endif %}
  • Submitted revisions, their repository evidence and review records are kept while the project is listed or under review. A project that was never accepted is kept until its owner or someone named in it asks us to remove it.
  • Deleted records can remain in Render's backups until those expire. Render and Cloudflare keep their own logs, which can include your IP address and the pages you request, under their own retention periods.

Who else handles it

Render hosts the site and database in Frankfurt on our instructions, with Cloudflare's network in front. Render and its sub-processors, including Cloudflare, Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud, are United States companies that can access the data from there; Render is certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and its UK Extension. Simon Gabriel lives in the United States and administers the site from there.

Your sign-in provider, GitHub and GitHub issues (if you propose a project through a public issue in our repository) are separate services under their own privacy policies. Our email delivery service sends the confirmations to contributors. Email to our contact address passes through our domain's forwarding service to our own mailboxes. We share nothing else unless the law requires it.

Your rights and how to use them

You can ask for a copy of your information, or ask us to correct it, delete it or restrict its use while a question is settled. You can also download your account data in a reusable format. Where we rely on legitimate interests, you can object at any time, on grounds relating to your situation, including to a public listing. We will then stop unless we have compelling grounds that override your interests, or need the information for legal claims. These rights have legal limits; if we cannot do everything you ask, we will tell you why. We reply within one month, or within three months for complex requests, in which case we tell you within the first month.

Signed-in users can open Your data and privacy requests to download their data or create a verified request file to send us. Anyone, with or without an account, can write to {% if privacy_contact %}{{ privacy_contact }}{% else %}our privacy contact{% endif %}.

If you are unhappy with how we use your information, please tell us first. You can also complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint, 0303 123 1113) or, in the European Union, to the data protection authority where you live or work.
