Information processed, purposes and legal bases

Account information. The controllers hold the identifier assigned by your sign-in provider (your ORCID iD, or your GitHub or GitLab account number), by which the website recognises you on later sign-ins. They also hold an internal account name generated at random, visible only to the controllers, the dates on which your account was created and last signed in, and a record of the projects you own. Submitting a project or an edit creates a fixed revision, which waits for repository verification when needed before proceeding to moderation. Older incomplete submissions remain available so that you may complete them. If you enable an authenticator, the website holds its encrypted secret, its recovery-code data and the dates of its use. The profile supplied by your provider and its access tokens are discarded, and your password remains with ORCID, GitHub, GitLab or, where offered, an institutional sign-in.{% if email_login_enabled %} If you sign in with email or add an email address, it is held for verification and account access and is never published.{% endif %} Purpose and legal basis: to provide the service for which you have registered, in performance of the controllers' contract with you (Article 6(1)(b) UK GDPR).

Organisation account applications. The controllers privately retain the organisation's name, account email, website and description, the representative's name and explanation of their authority, and the application date. A human moderator decides whether to activate the account; the decision, reason, moderator, date and decision-email delivery metadata are retained. These details support the account service described above and are not published in project listings. The organisation's account data download includes its application and decision. Applicants who cannot sign in may contact the controllers about their information.

Cookies. The website uses only cookies needed to run the service: a session cookie for signed-in users (up to 14 days), a cookie that protects forms against cross-site request forgery (up to one year) and a short-lived cookie that carries a status message to the next page. There is no "remember this browser" cookie, and no analytics or advertising cookies. Cookies of this kind are strictly necessary and do not require consent.

Security. The site uses technical information about visits and sign-ins to prevent abuse and protect accounts. Legal basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)).

Projects. Unpublished submissions and older incomplete submissions are visible only to the project's owner and the controllers. An accepted listing shows the title, description, contributor names and ORCID iDs, DOI, repository link and any project website. Every submitted revision and every moderation decision is retained. A contributor’s ORCID authentication status may also be public: it indicates control of that iD, not proof of their name, authorship or the mathematics. Where a contributor uses their connected ORCID, the controllers privately retain the identifier source and authentication/collection dates with the submission. The accepted abstract licence declaration and its date are also retained privately. A contributor's institutional email address is never published. It is visible only to the project's owner and the controllers, and is used to confirm a submission or revision to that contributor, and to contact them about the listing. A submitter may list only contributors whom they are authorised to name, and an ORCID iD in a contributor list does not give that person access to the submission. Purpose and legal basis: accurate and lasting scholarly attribution and an accountable record of moderation, in the legitimate interests of contributors, readers and the controllers (Article 6(1)(f)).

Repository authorization. The controllers hold the repository and owner identifiers, your GitHub account identifier, login name and permission level or the commit containing your verification file, any connected ORCID iD, and the dates of the check. This evidence is private. A verification file placed in a public repository is public there, including in the repository's history. When you verify a repository, when you submit a project and when a project is approved, the website queries GitHub about that public repository. An authorization granted through GitHub is used once and is not stored. Purpose and legal basis: publishing only projects that their submitters are authorised to list, in the controllers' legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)).

Privacy requests and security incidents. The controllers keep the correspondence and records needed to handle a request, a complaint or an incident. Purpose and legal basis: to meet the controllers' legal obligations (Article 6(1)(c)).

The controllers have assessed each of these legitimate interests against your interests and rights. The information involved is limited to what attribution and security require, and it is of the kind that researchers expect to accompany their work. The controllers do not use your information for marketing, do not sell it, and make no decision about you by automated means. You are not obliged to provide any information, but a project cannot be submitted without signing in and naming its contributors.

Contributors named by a submitter

A submitter may list you as a contributor to a project. The controllers then receive your name, your institutional email address and, where given, your ORCID iD from the submitter rather than from you, and process them as described under Projects above. When a submission is created or an edit is submitted, the website queues a separate confirmation to each distinct contributor email in that revision. These messages identify the project and invite replies about incorrect details; revision confirmations also give the revision number. Completing repository verification does not send another confirmation. Duplicate notices and excessive requests are limited to prevent email abuse; repeated revisions within an hour may not produce another email. Delivery is not guaranteed. You have the same rights as account holders, including the right to object, and no account is needed to exercise them.

Retention

  • Sign-in sessions last up to 14 days. Repository checks expire after ten minutes (GitHub authorization) or 24 hours (verification file), and a completed authorization is valid for 30 days.{% if email_login_enabled %} Email sign-in codes expire after three minutes.{% endif %}
  • An hourly cleanup removes expired sessions, and removes unused repository checks 30 days after they end.
  • Queued contributor notices expire after 24 hours. Delivery records, containing keyed address digests rather than copied email addresses, are removed after seven days. A limited set of digests stays with each submitted revision to prevent repeat notifications.
  • Older incomplete submissions that have never been submitted are deleted after 90 days without an edit or a repository check. Viewing a page does not extend this period.
  • Accounts with no projects or other responsibilities are deleted after twelve months without a sign-in. No reminder is sent beforehand.{% if email_login_enabled %} Unverified email registrations are deleted after seven days.{% endif %}
  • Organisation applications and their moderation records are retained for manual review, including pending or rejected applications. Accounts linked to these records are excluded from the automatic account-deletion rules above. Applicants may contact the controllers to request access, correction or removal.
  • Submitted revisions, their repository evidence and the associated moderation records are retained for as long as the project is listed or awaiting moderation. Never-accepted submissions are reviewed monthly once they have had no activity for six calendar months. If they are no longer needed, the owner is given at least 30 days’ notice before removal. A specific ongoing review, dispute or legal obligation may justify continued retention, with the reason recorded and reviewed again within three months. Previously published work is assessed separately. It is not removed automatically under this rule.
  • Deleted records may persist in Render's backups until those backups expire. Render and Cloudflare keep their own operational logs, which may include your IP address and the addresses of the pages you request, for their own retention periods.
