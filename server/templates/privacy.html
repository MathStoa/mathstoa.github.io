Privacy and your mathΣτοά account

mathΣτοά keeps a small amount of account information so contributors can submit projects and return to edit them.

Sign-in and private account information

We keep the identifier supplied by your sign-in provider, its connection to your mathΣτοά account, an internal account name generated at random (which the owners of projects you edit and staff may see), account creation and sign-in dates, and a record of which submissions your account may edit. We discard the provider's profile payload and do not retain its OAuth access or refresh tokens. When you sign in with ORCID, GitHub, GitLab or, where offered, an institutional sign-in, your password stays with that provider.

{% if email_login_enabled %}

If you choose email sign-in or add an email address to your account, we keep it for verification and account access. It is not included in the public archive.

{% else %}

Email sign-in is not currently offered, and mathΣτοά does not collect an email address when you sign in with a provider. The optional reply address in a privacy request file is the only address you can give us; it stays in that file and is not stored.

{% endif %}

mathΣτοά sets only the cookies needed to run the service: a sign-in session cookie (up to 14 days), a cross-site request forgery protection cookie that is set when a page contains a form, including the sign-in page, and lasts up to one year, and a short-lived cookie that carries a status message to the next page. There is no "remember this browser" cookie, and mathΣτοά sets no analytics or advertising cookies and loads no third-party scripts.

Temporary sign-in state, including pending email verification details and codes, is held in encrypted server-side sessions. If you enable an authenticator, we also retain its encrypted setup secret, recovery-code data and usage dates; no phone number is needed. Abuse prevention processes network and account identifiers; stored rate-limit cache keys use keyed digests rather than readable IP addresses or email identifiers.

Your sign-in provider operates its own service and privacy policy. Connecting an ORCID confirms control of that ORCID account; it does not certify authorship, employment or mathematical correctness.

Project details and attribution

Drafts and pending submissions are accessible to their owner, authorised editors and mathΣτοά staff. Accepted project descriptions, contributor names, ORCID identifiers, DOIs, repository links and any project website appear in the public archive.

Only include contributor details that you are authorised to publish. Institutional postal addresses and private contact details are not needed. An ORCID entered in a contributor list is supplied attribution; it does not automatically connect that person’s account or give them editing access.

Project repositories remain on their existing hosts. Their contents, access rules and privacy policies are managed there.

Repository authorization

To record permission to submit a repository, we retain the repository and owner identifiers, the account that verified access, dates and verification results. Depending on the method, this includes a GitHub account identifier, login name and permission level, or the commit identifying a verification file. A connected ORCID may be included. This evidence is private to account access and staff review; it is not part of the public listing.

A verification file that you place in a public repository is public there, including its submission identifier and any connected ORCID. Removing it from the current files may not remove it from the repository's history.

Editing and keeping a publication record

Submission revisions and review decisions record how a listing changes. An accepted version remains public while a revised submission is reviewed. Private invitation links grant editing access after the recipient signs in; share these links only with the intended collaborator.

Publicly available information about a person is still personal information. Account information and access records are kept separate from the public archive.

Expiry and retention

A sign-in session lasts up to 14 days from your sign-in or the last change to your session, unless you sign out. Email sign-in and verification codes expire after three minutes. Editor invitations expire after seven days. Repository verification challenges expire after ten minutes for GitHub authorization or 24 hours for a verification file. A completed repository authorization is valid for 30 days and is cancelled early if the repository, its owner or your connected ORCID changes; the record of the check is kept as described under Repository authorization. Expiry stops these credentials from being used; it does not by itself erase every stored record.

The maintenance cleanup removes expired sessions and database-cache entries, invitations that expired, were used or were revoked more than 30 days ago, and expired verification attempts created more than 30 days ago that have no completed proof. The standard deployment runs this cleanup at startup and hourly, retrying failed runs. Outages can delay removal.

Unverified email signups are removed after seven days if they have never signed in successfully, have no verified email or connected provider identity, and have no project responsibilities or other retained records.

Drafts that have never been submitted are removed after 90 days without a saved edit, editor-access change, invitation activity or repository-verification activity. Active invitations or verification work can delay removal. Opening a page alone does not restart this period. When such an unused draft is removed, its expired verification evidence and related private access records are also removed.

Accounts with no submissions, editing responsibilities or retained review/verification records are removed after 12 months (365 days) without a successful sign-in, measured from account creation if there has been no sign-in. Staff accounts and accounts with administrative roles or other retained records are excluded. We do not send reminder emails before automatic removal.

Published project records remain. Submitted revisions, including rejected and pending submissions, their verification evidence, and review and staff action records have no automatic deletion period. Account closure and changes to public scholarly attribution require separate handling; contact us to request access, correction or deletion.

mathΣτοά runs on Render, in its Frankfurt (Germany) region, with Cloudflare's network in front of it. They handle your connection and keep their own operational logs and backups, which can include your IP address and the full address of each page you request (including sign-in callback and invitation links), under their own retention settings. mathΣτοά's own request log records only the time, method, status and size of each request. A deleted database record may remain in a backup until that backup expires.

To verify a repository, and again each time a submission is sent for review or approved, mathΣτοά queries GitHub's API about that public repository. If you verify through GitHub, your temporary GitHub authorization is used once to confirm your account and permission level and is not stored.

Questions and corrections

If you have an account, open Your data and privacy requests to download your account data or create a verified access, correction or closure request. These actions require a recent sign-in and your authenticator when enabled. Downloads are generated without saving a server-side export file.

A verified request file contains your request, a confirmation linked to your account, and an optional reply address that is not added to your account. You send the file yourself to the privacy contact; mathΣτοά does not send an email or save the request as an account record. The confirmation lasts seven days and stops being valid after relevant account-security changes. Requests received in the contact mailbox are handled as private correspondence; account closure and deletion remain reviewed actions.
