About MathStoa

MathStoa is an archive of mathematical projects hosted in public Git repositories. It covers research, exposition, and formal proof projects, including work in progress.

Scope

A project may begin with a conjecture, a collection of examples, a reading programme, or an attempt to explain a difficult proof. MathStoa gives that work a public introduction: what it is about, where it stands, and where to read more.

The work stays in its own repository. The archive connects people to it. We welcome research, exposition, and formal proof projects, including projects that are incomplete.

The name brings together mathematics and stoa, a shared meeting place. Our wordmark, MathΣτοά, uses the Greek spelling of stoa.

Submission criteria

To be listed, a project should have:

  1. A public repository and a readable introduction to the mathematical question or purpose.
  2. Substantive material that another person can engage with: notes, examples, partial results, explanations, or formal work.
  3. A named steward who maintains the project or is authorised to submit it.
  4. A clear description of progress and whether contributions are welcome.
  5. Reuse terms and a contribution policy that explain how work is accepted and credited.

Submissions receive a basic editorial check for scope, clarity, and completeness. Listing does not certify mathematical correctness, establish originality, or constitute peer review. State any review or formal verification precisely, with a link to the relevant version.

A submission should identify the version of the work it describes; a stable archived snapshot is encouraged. The pilot announcement feed records accepted projects.

Contributions and credit

A useful correction, a new argument, an explanation, and project maintenance are different contributions. Record what each person did, including contributions made in discussion.

Section attribution, project maintenance, and authorship of a particular release or paper should be agreed separately. A small edit can be acknowledged without deciding the byline of a future paper; a short mathematical insight can deserve substantial credit.

Each project sets its policy before inviting contributions. Contributors should be able to review their attribution and raise disagreements with the named steward. MathStoa asks for transparent policies rather than using commit counts to assign credit.

Example entries

The initial entries are examples drawn from established public mathematics repositories. They illustrate the breadth of work that could belong here. They were selected for demonstration and have not been submitted or endorsed by their maintainers.

Example entries do not imply that a project is seeking collaborators, and they do not appear in the announcement feed. Visit the original repository for its current status, contributors, and participation rules.

As reviewed submissions arrive, the archive will distinguish those entries from examples.

Project and privacy

MathStoa is an independent experiment initiated by Tommaso Rosati. The website and archive are maintained in a public repository, and suggestions are welcome.

There is no tracking or advertising added by MathStoa. Visiting GitHub to submit a project is subject to GitHub’s own account and privacy policies. Submission drafts are prepared in your browser and are only sent to GitHub when you choose to open the review link.
